# Listing Tuner content and media production

**10 October 2026.** Listing Tuner is a Crankshaft managed service. All outputs are drafts until source facts, media rights, dealer approval and destination policies pass. Originals are immutable. The operator can explain exactly which verified input produced each claim or visual derivative.

## A. Listing text: from facts to approved copy

**Inputs.** Dealer's stock record, VIN, actual photographed equipment, price/fees, mileage date, title/condition disclosures, warranty/certification evidence, dealer style, destination limits and approved call to action. Map every input to source document, field, timestamp, and approval owner. VIN decode and market comps are cross-checks and context; they do not prove installed options or vehicle condition.

**Pipeline.** (1) Normalize units, stock lifecycle and source versions. (2) Flag conflicts and missing material facts. (3) Classify vehicle/buyer use case from verified facts only. (4) AI generates a structured object with `headline`, `summary`, `feature_bullets`, `disclosure_block`, `cta`, `claim_sources[]`, `questions_for_dealer[]`; each factual sentence links to fields. (5) Deterministic validator blocks absent provenance, mismatched price, unsupported safety/ownership/warranty/financing/accident claims, forbidden terms and destination length violations. (6) Human editor checks readability and factual meaning. (7) Dealer approves the version. (8) Destination adapter publishes and live monitor compares output to approved source.

**Copy rules.** Put identity, verified differentiators, mileage and price/fee clarity first. Use plain language and helpful buying context; avoid keyword stuffing and generic superlatives. Never infer accident-free, one-owner, service history, installed option, towing capacity, battery health, tax credit, financing approval or scarcity. If a fact is unknown, omit it and ask the dealer. Certification language only with current program proof. Preserve mandatory disclosures verbatim. Distinguish visible fact from derived interpretation: the description may say “hybrid” when verified, but not claim fuel savings without a supported figure.

**Evaluation.** Critical pass/fail: vehicle identity, stock, price, availability, mileage, seller, fees, condition, title/history, warranty, disclosures, contact path. A single material error blocks publication. Style score (clarity, specificity, ordering, length) is separate and cannot overcome a failed fact check. Log changes and use the same rubric before/after; do not call an AI rewrite an improvement solely because it is longer.

## B. Photos: improve presentation without changing evidence

**Intake.** Dealer uploads originals and rights statement per stock. Hash and archive originals; reject low resolution, wrong stock, duplicates, personal documents and images whose seller rights are unclear. Request a consistent 20–30 image shot set: four exterior corners, sides, front/rear, wheels/tires, cockpit, rear seats, odometer/dashboard, cargo, equipment and honest defect views. Preserve original EXIF privately where lawful; do not publish location metadata or personal information.

**Automated suggestions.** Computer vision groups angles, detects blur/glare/darkness, horizon tilt, excessive empty space, duplicate frames, plate/face/document exposure and likely mismatch with the stock vehicle. These are *flags*, not automatic truth claims. Prefer the clean three-quarter exterior as hero if available. Recommend an order that helps a buyer assess the actual vehicle, including visible wear and defects. A misleading crop that omits a known flaw from every photo is unacceptable.

**Permitted derivatives.** Rotate/straighten, modest crop while keeping the whole vehicle and condition cues visible, exposure/white-balance correction within a color-fidelity tolerance, moderate denoise/sharpen, resize/compress and face/plate privacy masking when appropriate. Keep clean, unbranded masters for platforms that prohibit overlays. Each derivative records original hash, crop rectangle, transform parameters, algorithm/version, reviewer and destination. Compare side by side at full size. Reject changes that alter paint color, hide damage, remove warning lights, change wheels/accessories, add reflections/shadows, replace backgrounds deceptively or hallucinate details. If the vehicle was physically cleaned or repaired, ask for a fresh photograph rather than editing the old one into a new condition.

**Sample gallery treatment.** The 40 local examples use observed thumbnail images with reversible browser presentation: consistent frame, aspect ratio, contained vehicle, dark neutral backdrop and adjusted crop where safe. The source bytes remain available alongside the preview; this is a visual merchandising proposal, **not** a claim that the underlying condition or pixels were edited. Full-resolution photo work requires dealer-supplied originals and rights.

## C. Video: Jev selects; a deterministic editor cuts

The user identified Jev as an instant-decision LLM. The public [HE Jev use case](https://www.hejev.com/ai-jev/) describes another model transcribing video and Jev scoring/ranking candidate moments; it does not describe Jev as a video editor. The published [TypeSafe API quickstart](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/quickstart) accepts typed `choice`/`score` questions at `/v1/systemone` with `model: "jev-latest"`. Build Jev as a low-latency **ranking service** and use FFmpeg or equivalent media software for actual cuts. Confirm account access, rate/rights and input limits before production.

**Inputs and preprocessing.** Use only client-supplied footage with permission. Ingest original file, stock match, checksum, orientation, frame rate, audio stream and duration. Generate timecoded speech transcript, shot/scene boundaries, representative frames, basic OCR (license plate, stock tags, screens), motion/blur/silence flags and vehicle-area detection. Human reviewer confirms any OCR/transcript that becomes factual text. Detect or redact people, plates, documents, addresses and copyrighted background music as required.

**Candidate generation.** Make candidate intervals from stable exterior and interior shots, walkaround highlights and narrated feature demonstrations. Discard intervals with wrong vehicle, obvious blur, transition artifacts, unsafe activity, unsupported claims, obscured damage or cut-off sentence. Target 15–30 second vertical clips and 30–60 second horizontal recaps as destination allows; preserve room for natural start/end and captions. Do not accelerate speech or cut a sentence into a false claim.

**Jev decision payload.** For each candidate send a compact *structured* representation: stock ID, verified facts allowed for the clip, interval timestamps, transcript excerpt, scene labels, quality scores, known defect/disclosure flags, and channel goal. Ask typed questions: relevance score 0–100, visual clarity score, factual-risk score, whether the candidate shows a distinct buyer-useful view, and best clip order. Jev's scores are advisory. The local policy engine rejects any candidate with a hard safety/factual flag regardless of rank, selects diverse views, and records prompt/model/version, scores and reason codes.

**Rendering and approval.** FFmpeg trims on exact timestamps, preserves source ratio or performs a safe crop/reframe, normalizes audio, makes captions from verified transcript, exports channel-specific H.264/AAC renditions and a contact sheet. Never generate synthetic vehicle frames or hide a defect with a cut. Reviewer watches entire final clip with audio and compares to source, checks captions and rights, then dealer approves. Store original, edit decision list, rendered files and approval. If Jev or transcript/OCR fails, a human editor can select clips manually; never invent a decision result.

**Metrics.** Track accepted candidate rate, editor minutes per finished clip, render failure, dealer rejection reason, clip completion and listing engagement where destination data is available. Test Jev against a human-ranked set before assuming it saves labor. The current business model's $10/clip editor allowance is a planning input that must be measured.
