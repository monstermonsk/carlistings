# Listing Tuner content and media production

**11 October 2026.** Listing Tuner is a Crankshaft managed service. All outputs are drafts until dealer source data/attestations, media rights, dealer approval and destination policies pass. Originals are immutable. The operator can explain which dealer statement or record produced each claim or visual derivative. The owner-approved [listing accuracy standard](./Listing-Accuracy-Standard.md) controls these workflows; Crankshaft is not an independent vehicle inspector.

## A. Listing text: from facts to approved copy

**Inputs.** Dealer's stock record, VIN, dealer-attested options and condition, price/fees, mileage date, dealer-designated required disclosures, warranty/certification statement, dealer style, destination limits and approved call to action. Map each factual field to a dealer statement/feed, timestamp, and approval owner. VIN decode and market comps are cross-checks and context; dealer attestation is sufficient for options and condition.

**Pipeline.** (1) Normalize units, stock lifecycle and source versions. (2) Flag direct conflicts and missing dealer-designated disclosure fields. (3) Classify vehicle/buyer use case from dealer-attested facts. (4) AI generates a structured object with `headline`, `summary`, `feature_bullets`, `disclosure_block`, `cta`, `claim_sources[]`, `questions_for_dealer[]`; a dealer attestation is a valid source. (5) Deterministic validator blocks known false statements, mismatched price, AI-invented safety/ownership/warranty/financing/accident claims, omitted dealer-required disclosures and destination length violations. (6) Human editor checks readability and factual meaning. (7) Dealer approves the version. (8) Destination adapter publishes and live monitor compares output to approved source.

**Copy rules.** Put identity, dealer-attested positives, mileage and price/fee clarity first. Use plain language and helpful buying context; avoid keyword stuffing and AI-invented superlatives. We may rely on the dealer's word for condition and options; do not infer accident-free, one-owner, service history, towing capacity, battery health, tax credit, financing approval or scarcity from a generic VIN decode or attractive photo. If the dealer has not supplied a fact, omit it or ask. Carry dealer-designated required disclosures into applicable channel versions. A red car is not described as gray.

**Evaluation.** Critical pass/fail: vehicle identity, stock, price, availability, mileage, seller, fees, condition, title/history, warranty, disclosures, contact path. A single material error blocks publication. Style score (clarity, specificity, ordering, length) is separate and cannot overcome a failed fact check. Log changes and use the same rubric before/after; do not call an AI rewrite an improvement solely because it is longer.

## B. Photos: improve presentation without changing evidence

**Intake.** Dealer uploads originals and rights statement per stock. Hash and archive originals; reject low resolution, wrong stock, duplicates, personal documents and images whose seller rights are unclear. Request a consistent 20–30 image shot set: four exterior corners, sides, front/rear, wheels/tires, cockpit, rear seats, odometer/dashboard, cargo, equipment and dealer-requested condition views. Preserve original EXIF privately where lawful; do not publish location metadata or personal information.

**Automated suggestions.** Computer vision groups angles, detects blur/glare/darkness, horizon tilt, excessive empty space, duplicate frames, plate/face/document exposure and likely mismatch with the stock vehicle. These are presentation flags, not independent condition judgments. Prefer the clean three-quarter exterior as hero if available. Choose attractive real angles and include any condition views the dealer designates; the listing is not required to photograph every flaw.

**Permitted derivatives.** Rotate/straighten, crop to improve composition, adjust lighting/white balance while retaining the actual vehicle color, denoise and sharpen to any useful strength, resize/compress and mask private information when appropriate. AI may recommend edit parameters, but listing photos stay derivatives of real captures: no generative replacement of the vehicle, inpainting of damage, synthetic background or composite of another car. Keep clean, unbranded masters for platforms that prohibit overlays. Each derivative records original hash, crop rectangle, transform parameters, algorithm/version, reviewer and destination. Compare side by side at full size. Reject digital changes that turn a red car gray, remove dents or damage, or change wheels/accessories or condition. Selective framing is allowed; changing the vehicle itself is not.

**Sample gallery treatment.** The 40 local examples use observed thumbnail images with reversible browser presentation: consistent frame, aspect ratio, contained vehicle, dark neutral backdrop and adjusted crop where safe. The source bytes remain available alongside the preview; this is a visual merchandising proposal, **not** a claim that the underlying condition or pixels were edited. Full-resolution photo work requires dealer-supplied originals and rights.

## C. Video: Jev selects; a deterministic editor cuts

The user identified Jev as an instant-decision LLM. The public [HE Jev use case](https://www.hejev.com/ai-jev/) describes another model transcribing video and Jev scoring/ranking candidate moments; it does not describe Jev as a video editor. The published [TypeSafe API quickstart](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/quickstart) accepts typed `choice`/`score` questions at `/v1/systemone` with `model: "jev-latest"`. Build Jev as a low-latency **ranking service** and use FFmpeg or equivalent media software for actual cuts. Confirm account access, rate/rights and input limits before production.

**Inputs and preprocessing.** Use only client-supplied footage with permission. Ingest original file, stock match, checksum, orientation, frame rate, audio stream and duration. Generate timecoded speech transcript, shot/scene boundaries, representative frames, basic OCR (license plate, stock tags, screens), motion/blur/silence flags and vehicle-area detection. Human reviewer confirms any OCR/transcript that becomes factual text. Detect or redact people, plates, documents, addresses and copyrighted background music as required.

**Candidate generation.** Make candidate intervals from stable exterior and interior shots, walkaround highlights and dealer-attested feature demonstrations. Discard intervals with wrong vehicle, obvious blur, transition artifacts, unsafe activity, known false claims or cut-off sentences. Target 15–30 second vertical clips and 30–60 second horizontal recaps as destination allows; preserve room for natural start/end and captions. Do not cut a sentence into a false claim. Clips can focus on positives without showing every condition issue.

**Jev decision payload.** For each candidate send a compact *structured* representation: stock ID, verified facts allowed for the clip, interval timestamps, transcript excerpt, scene labels, quality scores, known defect/disclosure flags, and channel goal. Ask typed questions: relevance score 0–100, visual clarity score, factual-risk score, whether the candidate shows a distinct buyer-useful view, and best clip order. Jev's scores are advisory. The local policy engine rejects any candidate with a hard safety/factual flag regardless of rank, selects diverse views, and records prompt/model/version, scores and reason codes.

**Rendering and approval.** FFmpeg trims on exact timestamps, preserves source ratio or performs a safe crop/reframe, normalizes audio, makes captions from a checked transcript, exports channel-specific H.264/AAC renditions and a contact sheet. Do not generate synthetic vehicle frames or change the meaning of a spoken claim. Reviewer watches the final clip with audio and checks captions/rights, then dealer approves. Store original, edit decision list, rendered files and approval. If Jev or transcript/OCR fails, a human editor can select clips manually; never invent a decision result.

**Metrics.** Track accepted candidate rate, editor minutes per finished clip, render failure, dealer rejection reason, clip completion and listing engagement where destination data is available. Test Jev against a human-ranked set before assuming it saves labor. The current business model's $10/clip editor allowance is a planning input that must be measured.
