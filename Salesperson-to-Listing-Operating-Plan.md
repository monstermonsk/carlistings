# Crankshaft Listing Tuner: salesperson-to-listing operating plan

**11 October 2026 · Build blueprint.** The salesperson's recurring task is to identify the vehicle, take photos and a phone video, and upload them. Crankshaft does the research, editing, writing, quality control, channel formatting, publishing and measurement. Listing Tuner is a Crankshaft service. Accuracy follows the owner-approved [listing accuracy standard](./Listing-Accuracy-Standard.md): rely on dealer statements, highlight positives, and do not make obvious claims or edits we know are false. Nothing in this document implies that an integration or neural network is already running.

## Product objective: the best feasible listing for this car, here, now

For each stock lifecycle and destination, assemble the **best listing we can measure for that exact vehicle in its local market** under the proposed accuracy standard. “Best” is not a universal template. It depends on the unit's dealer-attested equipment and condition, the local competing inventory, the dealer's price and margin goals, the channel's format and the response of qualified shoppers. The system should be automatic from the salesperson's point of view, with an operator and dealer approval gate during the launch phase. As audited error rates fall, the dealer may authorize straight-through publication for defined low-risk changes; material factual claims and condition edits stay reviewable.

The optimization is constrained before it is scored: correct unit, dealer-attested claims, no known falsehood or fabricated vehicle media, dealer-designated disclosures, authorized media, current price/status and destination rules. Among candidates that pass, rank **expected qualified appointments per relevant exposure**, use verified sale probability and gross/holding economics as longer-term outcomes, and respect each dealer's chosen priorities. Because outcomes are sparse, start with editorial and market-matched models, then replace guesses with partner and dealer funnel data. Show confidence and the reason for every suggestion. Never call a version “best” solely because the model gives it a high aesthetic score.

```text
Salesperson captures stock + photos + video
  → collect dealer attestations for unit facts, options and disclosures
  → build local comparison set and buyer-demand baseline
  → identify gaps versus strong comparable listings
  → make safe photo order/crops, copy, and clip candidates
  → filter truth, policy, rights, and mobile usability
  → predict qualified-buyer response for this car/market/channel
  → dealer-approved publish through inventory software and feeds
  → measure listing, call, appointment, sale, and gross outcomes
  → test and refresh the version when evidence says to improve it
```

The **local comparison set** should match category/body, make/model/trim or use case, year band, mileage, condition, price band, market radius and channel. Dynamically widen the radius or model family when data is sparse, with lower confidence shown. From licensed partner snapshots, compute price position, local supply, turnover, typical angle coverage, copy specificity, video prevalence, qualified-lead rate and sale hazard. Separate the car's inherent demand and dealer price from the *editable presentation*: photo choice/order, verified headline/body, clip choice and disclosure clarity. The action engine should recommend an edit only when it is feasible with existing evidence and its predicted benefit exceeds uncertainty and production cost. Otherwise it asks for a precise new shot or fact, or tells the dealer that price/stock factors dominate and require their decision.

The recommendation record is `stock_lifecycle + market_snapshot + channel + approved_facts + original_media_hashes + candidate_version + model_version + expected_outcome + uncertainty + reason_codes + approval + observed_outcome`. Recompute on a material change: price, mileage, condition, new media, nearby comparable supply, platform format, or declining relative performance. Keep the previous approved version and rollback path. The system learns from edits and experiments while the dealer sees a simple answer: what changed, why, and whether it produced more qualified buyers.

## 1. The field workflow: a guided capture target under 15 minutes

The mobile capture page (a browser-based PWA first) opens a dealer stock number. The salesperson scans the windshield VIN or types it, confirms the decoded year/make/model against the car, and enters the odometer reading. If the DMS or inventory feed already has stock, price and disclosures, these fields are shown read-only or as discrepancy prompts. The salesperson only confirms that the media is the same unit and that the dealer may use it. Do not make the salesperson write ad copy, name options or edit video. Measure capture time during the pilot; the under-15-minute target depends on vehicle size, condition and network quality.

The app guides **16 standard views**: front-left and rear-right three-quarter views; front, rear, both sides; four wheels/tires; driver cockpit; center controls; front and rear seating; odometer; cargo/bed. Add category-specific views and dealer-designated condition/equipment views. Aim for 20–30 photos, with a minimum resolution gate. The prompts show a silhouette for each angle, give immediate feedback for severe blur/darkness/wrong orientation, and allow a retake on the spot. The dealer decides which condition issues require listing disclosure; Crankshaft does not digitally erase issues visible in selected source media.

Record one or two 45–120 second walkarounds in the phone's native camera: a slow full exterior pass, then interior/cargo/features. Pause at each corner; keep the car fully in frame; hold the phone steady; speak only facts the salesperson knows. Vertical video serves Shorts/Reels/TikTok; a separate horizontal pass can serve the vehicle detail page. If only one orientation is available, the editor must make a safe crop or use letterboxing. The app uploads in resumable chunks, verifies checksums, then reports missing views or unusable footage. A rushed capture is allowed as a draft; it enters a retake queue rather than silently becoming a weak publication.

`capture.completed` creates an immutable media batch with tenant, stock lifecycle, uploader, capture time, rights attestation, SHA-256 hashes and originals. Strip public GPS/serial metadata from derivatives. Restrict raw originals to the tenant and editors.

## 2. Vehicle facts: free lookup, evidence and exact options

Call the **free NHTSA vPIC `DecodeVinValuesExtended` API** with VIN and model year, cache by VIN/year, respect its rate controls, and save the raw response and timestamp. It supplies manufacturer-submitted VIN structure data such as year, make, model, body and some powertrain attributes. It is a cross-check on identity. A blank decoded field does not prove absence of a feature; a decoded trim does not prove every package on this unit. NHTSA also offers batch decoding and a downloadable database for later scale. [Official vPIC API](https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/), [NHTSA decoder guidance](https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/decoder/).

Use this operational source order per **claim**, with dealer attestation sufficient for condition and options:

| Claim | Primary proof | Secondary check | If missing/conflicting |
|---|---|---|---|
| VIN, year, make, model | Physical VIN plus DMS/stock record | vPIC decode | Hold listing and ask dealer. |
| Price, miles, availability, fees, title/disclosures | Current dealer inventory/DMS and signed policy | Staff confirmation | Hold or omit the claim; recheck before publish. |
| Installed option/package | Named dealer attestation or authorized inventory feed | Optional OEM build sheet, invoice/option codes or clear photo | Ask the dealer about a direct conflict; generic VIN data alone is only a candidate. |
| Condition, accident/history, certification, warranty | Dealer attestation and its disclosure payload | Optional inspection/history/program documents | Use dealer wording; ask the dealer about a direct conflict. Do not infer from appearance or VIN. |
| Fuel economy | EPA configuration matched to the exact verified variant | Dealer build record | Omit the number if configuration remains ambiguous. |

The [EPA FuelEconomy.gov web services](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/ws/index.shtml) are free for model/configuration lookup and useful after configuration is confirmed; they do not identify installed options from VIN. The dealer's unit-specific attestation is enough for option claims under the proposed standard. Window stickers, OEM feeds and paid build data are optional enrichers and cross-checks, not publication prerequisites. Store `fact_value`, `source`, `observed_at`, `dealer_attestor`, `approval_state` and conflicts. Photo OCR and visual recognition may suggest a feature, but only create a review task until the dealer confirms it.

**Decoder provider ladder and budget.** vPIC is the free baseline. [Wenmar Open](https://open.wenmarpro.com/docs) advertises a free no-key decoder and catalog, but its published data version says it derives from NHTSA vPIC, so it is a convenient interface or fallback, **not independent proof of options**. Ask each dealer which VIN-decoding, window-sticker, OEM portal or DMS subscriptions it already has and whether its agreement permits a third-party integration. Add a provider adapter only after reviewing API terms, allowed storage, per-decode fees, field coverage, source provenance and dealer authorization. [DataOne](https://www.dataonesoftware.com/web-services-vin-decoder-api) offers option-level and OEM-build products; [Auto.dev OEM Build Data](https://docs.auto.dev/v2/products/oem-build-data) advertises factory-option data. These are candidates to price and test, not assumed free or subscribed. A sample of VINs across makes/years should compare installed-option accuracy against dealer build sheets before purchase. Cache approved results when contractually allowed, avoid duplicate paid requests for the same lifecycle, and show the expected lookup cost in the internal unit-economics ledger. Never promote “available on this trim” into “installed on this unit.”

## 3. Photo improvement as a reproducible pipeline

1. **Match and inspect.** Associate photos with one stock lifecycle. Compare visible VIN/stock tags and vehicle appearance to the record; quarantine mixed-car batches. Compute dimensions, aspect, orientation, duplicate/perceptual-hash groups, blur (variance of Laplacian plus a learned score), clipping/brightness histograms, glare, tilt, subject size and occlusion. Flag people, plates, documents and location-sensitive details for privacy review. These thresholds are camera and category dependent, so calibrate them on dealer-authorized examples.
2. **Classify views and completeness.** Classify each image into front-left, rear-right, side, front/rear, wheel, interior, dash, odometer, cargo and condition/other. Keep multiple useful views even if visually similar; suppress near duplicates that add no new information. A missing hero or odometer generates a retake request; a condition view is requested when the dealer marks it as required.
3. **Rank for buyer usefulness.** Hard filters reject wrong vehicle, severe blur, unreadable odometer or an edit that makes an obvious false depiction. A model can rank remaining images within each view class for clarity, framing and subject coverage. Select an appealing set with an exterior hero, both sides, interior and equipment; add condition views when the dealer requests them. The goal is attractive merchandising of the actual unit, not an exhaustive inspection gallery.
4. **Polish real photos.** Apply EXIF rotation, lens correction if measured, horizon straightening, lighting/white-balance correction, denoise, sharpening, useful crop, then resize/compress for each channel. Sharpen as much as improves presentation; there is no fixed strength cap. AI can choose parameters, but the output remains an edit of the real capture, not an AI-created vehicle, synthetic background or composite. Keep the actual paint and upholstery colors; a red car must not become gray. Keep overlays off clean marketplace masters. No digital dent/damage removal or changes to body, badges, wheels, accessories or condition. Compare the export with its original at full size.
5. **Verify.** Record original hash, transform parameters, software version, output hash and reviewer. Present original/derivative side by side at 100% zoom. Fail if the edit digitally removes damage or changes paint/parts/condition from the chosen source image. Cropping and selecting better angles are normal merchandising; the dealer's required disclosures must still appear in the applicable listing. An editor approves the set, then the dealer approves the publication version.

Output separate high-resolution master, channel sizes and accessible alt text based only on verified vehicle identity and visible angle. If the shot itself is beyond repair, request a retake; software cannot restore genuine detail from blur or compression.

## 4. Turning weak phone footage into short-form clips

The ingest worker probes codec, frame rate, rotation, duration, audio, resolution and corrupt frames with `ffprobe`. It transcribes speech with timestamps, detects shot boundaries, samples keyframes, and estimates sharpness, exposure, steadiness, subject position, motion, noise and music/people/plate risk. It links each scene to the same stock record and approved facts. A scene that shows a different car, unsafe driving, an unsupported spoken claim or illegible feature label is held for review.

Make candidate intervals around useful moments: opening three-quarter exterior, full walkaround, cabin, controls, rear seats, cargo/bed, and positive dealer-attested features. Require stable lead-in/out and a complete spoken thought. Build a shot graph so each finished clip tells one small story rather than repeating the same angle. Suggested outputs per usable shoot: one 20–30 second overview, two to four 10–25 second feature clips, and one 30–60 second horizontal recap when footage supports them. These are targets, not quotas: if only one segment is usable, publish one or ask for a retake.

**Jev's possible role is ranking candidate moments.** Send Jev a small structured choice or score request containing interval timestamps, checked transcript, visual-quality scores, scene type, approved facts and channel goal. Jev can suggest which intervals are interesting and distinct; a deterministic policy still rejects factual/rights/quality failures, and FFmpeg or another editor performs the actual cut. The public [HE Jev use case](https://www.hejev.com/ai-jev/) describes a preceding transcription model and Jev ranking moments, not Jev editing video. Confirm API access, pricing, input format and reliability in a pilot before depending on it.

For each selected clip, create an edit decision list `{source_hash, in_ms, out_ms, crop_track, captions, audio_gain, render_profile}`. Track the vehicle in frame and reframe to 9:16 only where the full car or intended detail remains intelligible; otherwise use letterbox/pillarbox. Apply mild stabilization, measured exposure/white balance, limited denoise and audio normalization; do not fake frames or erase scratches/dents. Generate captions from corrected speech, omit copyrighted background music unless licensed, add a verified stock/CTA slate only where the destination permits it, render H.264/AAC and inspect the **entire** export on a phone. Human review compares each clip to source and confirms no context changed. [FFprobe](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html), [FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html).

Retake triggers: wrong car, mostly blurred footage, resolution too low for the requested crop, unsafe/rights issue, no coherent stable interval, spoken misinformation that cannot be cut without misleading context, or no useful view of the vehicle. The operator asks for specific missing shots, not a vague “better video.”

## 5. Copy, approval and publishing without salesperson labor

Merge the dealer's current inventory feed, vPIC cross-check, dealer-attested options/condition, price/mileage and required disclosure payload into a canonical stock record. Generate a structured listing with headline, 2–4 sentence summary, feature bullets, disclosure block, CTA and `claim_sources[]`; a dealer attestation is a valid source. The validator rejects known contradictions, stale price/status, AI-invented history/warranty/safety claims, omitted dealer-required disclosures, and channel length/policy violations. An editor checks meaning, then the dealer's designated approver accepts the listing and media bundle. Publish through the dealer's authorized inventory syndicator/CMS/marketplace route; use a human portal task where no authorized API exists. Fetch the live page or destination acknowledgement and compare VIN/stock, price, hero, copy, availability and call destination. Changes to source data create a new version and reapproval task. A sold event withdraws or queues withdrawal.

The salesperson sees a simple status: `uploaded → needs retake / researching → in review → approved → live`, with precise action requests only when evidence is missing. Crankshaft staff own exception handling and editing. The dealer retains final control of factual claims and publishing permissions.

### Dealer software and platform delivery contract

Onboarding inventories **both** the system of record (DMS, inventory manager or syndicator) and every desired destination. The dealer identifies which system currently sends feeds to its website and marketplaces, who can authorize read/write access, what content fields are accepted, and which source wins when two systems update one stock. Crankshaft first writes the approved listing and media to the dealer's existing **syndication hub** when that hub already controls downstream inventory; direct marketplace publishing is an adapter only when the dealer has an authorized route. Fortellis's [API directory](https://docs.fortellis.io/docs/general/api-directory-marketplace/api-directory/) shows that API access and pricing plans depend on the specific provider and subscription; do not assume a universal DMS endpoint.

Each destination adapter implements `capabilities()`, `upsert(approved_version)`, `remove(stock_lifecycle)`, `get(external_id)` and `acknowledge(event)`. Capabilities specify field lengths, aspect ratios, image/video limits, disclosure fields, stock ID format, update latency, cost, and whether a dealer approval token is required. Use OAuth/API credentials, SFTP/CSV/XML feeds or a supervised portal workflow as authorized. Never use marketplace scraping as a publishing interface. On an upsert, map the canonical record to destination fields, validate before sending, attach idempotency key and source version, save external ID/receipt, then check the live page or returned listing. Alert on mismatched price, sold status, wrong hero/media order, stripped disclosures, broken lead/call route or stale version. Failed writes retry with backoff and enter an operator queue; do not mark them live. A sold signal preempts queued upserts and triggers removals across all destinations. Keep a per-dealer integration map and test one real stock end to end before adding another platform.

## 6. What “best listing” means: measure results, not taste

Create an analysis row for **listing × channel × day × stock lifecycle**. Capture impressions, vehicle-detail views, saves, leads, qualified calls, appointments, showings, sales and time-to-sale where the dealer/channel provides them. Tag paid spend, placement, price changes, days in stock, vehicle age/mileage/trim, local market and season. Treat known falsehoods, fabricated vehicle media and missing dealer-required disclosures as hard gates; then compare engagement rates per impression and qualified appointments per vehicle-detail view. A polished photo that digitally misrepresents the actual unit is a failed listing.

Start with a human audit rubric across identity/accuracy, photo coverage/quality, text specificity, video usefulness, disclosures and contact path. The existing 40 public examples are a diagnostic gallery only: marketplace rights and missing outcome data make them unsuitable as a training set or proof of conversion lift. Obtain dealer-authorized originals plus outcomes from pilots. For the first model, target a few hundred vehicle sets and thousands of photo-pair judgments, then expand based on validation performance. Log who labeled each pair and why, including “neither is usable.”

**Training-data acquisition.** With signed partner download and model-use rights, collect a large multi-site corpus of listing text, photos, video and lifecycle updates through feeds or authorized crawlers. Attach the rights record, source URL, capture date, permitted uses and takedown route to each asset. Combine it with dealer-authorized originals, approved edits, editor preferences and downstream outcomes. Deduplicate by VIN, image hash and perceptual similarity, then split whole dealers/vehicles across train and holdout. The visible listing corpus teaches presentation patterns; exposure and sales-funnel data teach which patterns actually work.

### Optimistic partner-data program: licensed bulk acquisition

The target scenario assumes the founder secures **download, processing, model-training and derivative-model rights** from listing-site owners through revenue-share deals. Build a partner ingestion service that accepts an official bulk feed/API when available and a permitted crawler when the partner authorizes it. The agreement should spell out domains/endpoints, crawl windows and rate limits, media resolution, historical depth, updates and sold events, retention/deletion, model-weight survival after contract end, redistribution of example images, and whether site-owner/dealer/photographer rights are all covered. Treat partner revenue share as a separate data-acquisition cost in unit economics; do not change the dealer's plan-minute billing to fund it.

Planning scale for the first broad corpus: **100,000–250,000 licensed listing lifecycles**, several million images, and as many usable videos as partners provide. This is a procurement target, not a claim of access. Every snapshot gets `partner_id`, `source_listing_id`, `VIN/stock` when supplied, capture time, image/video hashes, raw text, price, market/channel, rights record and lifecycle state. Incremental jobs capture changes and removal notices. Deduplicate reposted inventory across sites and prevent one car's photos from leaking across training/validation/test. Store raw copies in restricted object storage, structured records in a warehouse and a searchable review sample; support partner deletion requests and lineage back to every derived training row.

Negotiate **impressions, detail-page views, saves, lead/contact events, qualified calls, appointments and sold status** in the same deal, preferably aggregated or pseudonymous at listing/day level. This turns the corpus into evidence about buyer response. If a partner can license only visible listings and media, use that data for representation learning, view/quality classification and human-preference labeling; use dealer/site partners with outcomes for conversion modeling. Do not label the most viewed listing “best” without accounting for placement, paid spend, price, market and stock age. Build partner-specific holdouts, a future-time test set and randomized tests on owned dealer pages. Publish a model card with data coverage and measured gains by dealer, vehicle segment and channel. The commercial upside of licensed scale is broader visual coverage and faster learning; the decision to roll out a model still rests on audited factual accuracy and measured dealer outcomes.

Where the dealer website or ad account supports experiments, randomize one change at a time (hero photo, photo order, copy, clip) within comparable eligible inventory and preselect a primary metric, such as qualified appointments per vehicle-detail view. Keep price and paid promotion stable or record changes. On channels without randomized control, use matched cohorts and report the result as observational; model adjustments cannot turn it into a causal claim. Review statistical uncertainty and dealer-level variation before rolling out a “winner.” [Google Ads experiments guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7281575?hl=en), [Google Ads API experiments](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/experiments/overview).

### Public-web demand signals and listing age

Your key hypothesis is useful: **a car still listed much longer than comparable cars is likely generating weaker buyer demand.** Measure it as a relative signal, controlling for market and car type rather than applying a universal “30 days is bad” rule. For every licensed listing, collect daily `first_seen`, `last_confirmed_active`, asking price and price changes, stock/VIN, dealer, location, make/model/trim, year, miles, condition/certification, media/copy version, category, channel, promotion/placement when visible, and `sold`, `withdrawn`, `transferred` or `unknown` exit state. Merge cross-posts and relists into one vehicle lifecycle; otherwise a relisted slow seller looks new. A disappearance alone is **not** a confirmed sale.

Compare each active car with a local cohort of similar body style, model/trim, age, mileage, condition and price band during the same season. Public features include relative asking price, local supply of close substitutes, arrival/removal rate, listing age percentile, frequency and size of price cuts, media completeness, days since last update, dealer stock turnover, and visible save/view/comment counts if the site exposes them under the data deal. Search-interest trends for a model/region can be a segment-level prior. None of these identifies an individual qualified buyer; they estimate demand around the vehicle and listing.

Fit a **daily time-to-event model**: among listings active at day `t`, estimate the chance of a confirmed sale in the next 7/30 days. Treat still-active listings as right-censored and separate confirmed sale from withdrawal, auction transfer, expired ad or unknown disappearance. Include listing age, market, car type, price relative to comparable units, dealer, channel, season and paid placement as controls. Then calculate a listing's *observed demand gap*: expected sell-through for its cohort versus its actual age, engagement and lead rate. A low relative sale hazard plus weak engagement is a useful review queue for price, photos and copy; it is not proof that photo quality caused the delay. Survival analysis has been applied to used-car pricing and demand in published research. [Born et al., Price Management in the Used-Car Market](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/230775/1/irtg1792dp2018-065.pdf).

To learn about **qualified buyers**, request partner/dealer funnel labels: vehicle-detail views → contact attempts → reached contacts → verified purchase intent → appointments → showings → sales. A qualified-buyer label needs a real interaction and agreed rule, such as an in-market shopper asking about the unit, reachable callback or completed appointment; public browsing history or a save alone is an *intent proxy*. Use aggregated or pseudonymous counts in modeling. Analyze both `qualified leads / detail-page views` and `sales / qualified leads` so a listing that attracts curiosity but not serious shoppers is not called a winner. The call verifier and CRM provide the stronger ground-truth labels; the public web corpus provides scale and market context.

### A fuller listing-quality scorecard

Keep **publication accuracy** separate from performance. A wrong VIN/car, option the dealer has not attested, knowingly stale price, fabricated vehicle edit, omitted dealer-required disclosure, bad media rights or broken call route fails the listing regardless of click-through rate. For listings that pass, report these dimensions independently before collapsing them into a calibrated ranking:

| Dimension | Observable measures | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer comprehension | Blinded five-second test: can shoppers identify vehicle, price, miles, key differentiator, condition and next step?; repeated clarification questions on calls | Whether the ad answers practical questions rather than merely looking polished. |
| Evidence coverage | Useful angle coverage, readable odometer/tires/interior, dealer-designated condition views, dealer-attested options, original-to-derivative consistency | Whether a buyer can assess the actual unit and trust the presentation. |
| Presentation craft | Hero photo quality, duplicate rate, mobile crop safety, image load time, photo order, readable copy, useful first sentence, captioned clips and clip completion | Whether the content is easy to inspect on the destination device. |
| Search fit | Eligible search impressions, rank/placement, click-through per impression, query-to-vehicle match, feed acceptance and update latency | Whether the right shoppers can find the car; paid placement must be recorded. |
| Intent and conversion | Detail-page views, photo-gallery depth, saves, contact clicks, reached leads, qualified calls, appointments, showings and sales, each divided by its relevant upstream exposure | Whether attention becomes real shopping behavior. |
| Sale economics | Sale probability by day, actual gross margin, discount from initial ask, holding days/cost, and return or complaint signals where available | Whether a fast sale created business value and the listing set honest expectations. |

Use a provisional **editorial readiness grade** for internal QA: correct unit, current dealer terms and dealer-designated disclosures are pass/fail; score applicable photo coverage/clarity, dealer-sourced copy, video usefulness, price/fee clarity, and contact path against a written rubric. Hide or reweight unavailable metrics rather than awarding a false zero. Have two reviewers independently grade a sample and measure agreement. Run blinded same-vehicle pairwise tests with shoppers: “Which version tells you enough to decide whether to contact the dealer, and why?” That is stronger than a generic attractiveness vote.

Once partner funnel data arrives, learn an **outcome index** relative to expected performance for the same car type, market, price position, channel and exposure. Show both the predicted improvement and observed result with uncertainty. Track funnel leakage: high clicks but low qualified calls can indicate curiosity, unclear facts or misleading images; many qualified calls but few appointments can indicate availability/price/service problems beyond the listing. Ask the verifier to code repeated listing questions and mismatches, then feed them back to the editor. Validate any score against later holdout periods and randomized edits. Do not optimize click-through alone, and do not reward a deep discount or paid boost as if it were copy quality.

## 7. PyTorch model: start with a ranker, then learn outcomes

The first useful neural net is a **photo selection assistant**, not an end-to-end listing generator. Fine-tune a pretrained MobileNetV3-Small encoder with a view-class head and a scalar usefulness head. Add measurable metadata (blur, exposure, subject coverage) to the learned image representation. Train on dealer-authorized pairs of photos from the **same stock and view class**, labeled “A better,” “B better,” or “both unusable.” Pairwise ranking loss encourages the preferred photo to score higher; classification loss teaches angle coverage. Use hard policy gates separately. [Torchvision MobileNetV3](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/main/models/generated/torchvision.models.mobilenet_v3_small.html), [PyTorch transfer learning](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/transfer_learning_tutorial).

Split by VIN/stock lifecycle and dealer, with a later time holdout, so near-duplicate photos of one vehicle never appear in both train and test. Report pairwise accuracy, view-class recall, severe-blur miss rate, defect-view retention, wrong-stock false acceptance and calibration by phone model/light conditions. Require a human override and log it for retraining. Do not auto-publish from model score.

For video, first use deterministic scene detection plus a small ranker of keyframe embeddings and numeric motion/audio/transcript features. Train it against human editor choices only after gathering licensed footage and accepted/rejected intervals. Compare Jev ranking to that baseline on the same held-out clips for editor time saved and factual rejection rate. Build a listing-level outcome predictor only after enough first-party exposure/conversion data exists; use it for prioritizing experiments, not as a substitute for randomized evidence. Version training data, model weights, thresholds and evaluation reports.

**Scale-up architecture once partner data arrives.** Train image and text encoders on the licensed corpus to learn vehicle views, photo quality and listing language; supervised labels still distinguish accurate detail from attractive but misleading presentation. Represent a listing as a set of image embeddings with angle/condition coverage, a text embedding, video-clip quality/coverage, verified facts and channel context. A small attention/set model in PyTorch predicts *rates* for detail-page views, qualified contacts and appointments using impression or view counts as denominators; train with exposure-aware likelihood/loss and masks for missing outcomes. A second daily hazard head predicts confirmed sale versus other exit while accounting for active right-censored cars. Include price position, vehicle segment, dealer, channel, geography, paid placement and days in stock as controls. Hold out entire dealers, VIN lifecycles, partner sites and future months. Compare with a simple regularized tabular baseline; keep the neural model only if it improves calibration and ranking on those holdouts. Explain recommendations as observable changes (missing rear-seat view, blurry hero, weak first sentence) and require the hard factual/media gates even when a model predicts lift. Use randomized site experiments to validate any claimed conversion gain. This makes the large licensed scrape valuable while avoiding a model that merely learns which dealer buys more advertising.

The repository's [`ml/`](./ml/README.md) directory contains a **starter implementation**, not trained weights: a free vPIC lookup adapter, a PyTorch photo ranker and a pairwise training script. It has no dealer media or proprietary data. Install the ML dependencies on the developer's machine and train only after rights, labels and holdout data are available.

## 8. Extend the asset schema by category

Use `AssetIdentity {category, identifier_type, identifier, stock_id, manufacturer, model, model_year, variant}` and category adapters for shot checklists, facts, claim rules, pricing units and channels. Cars and road trucks use VIN/vPIC. Motorhomes, trailers and motorcycles may decode through vPIC but need category-specific dealer attestations for equipment and condition. Boats use a **Hull Identification Number (HIN)** and manufacturer identification code, not an automotive VIN; there is no verified free universal boat-options API. RV interiors, trailer axles, boat hours/engines and powersports accessories need their own checklists and attestation fields. Share ingestion, provenance, media QA, approval and publishing infrastructure; train or adapt category-specific heads before applying car image scores to boats or RVs. [USCG manufacturer/HIN resources](https://www.uscgboating.org/manufacturers/index.php).

## 9. Build sequence and acceptance gates

**Pilot 1:** mobile upload, stock/VIN match, vPIC cache, immutable originals, rule-based photo QA, manually edited clips, structured copy and dealer approval for one dealer and one authorized publishing route.
